## 1.1.2 Everything is Online

The internet has become an essential part of everyday life. Smartphones, tablets, laptops and desktop computers use the internet to communicate, share information, access services and learn.

The internet allows people and devices to remain connected across the world.

## 1.1.3 Who Owns "The Internet"?

Nobody owns the entire internet.

The internet is a worldwide collection of interconnected networks that cooperate to exchange information using common standards.

This is why the internet is also called a **Network of Networks**.

### Communication Media

Information travels through different communication media:

- Telephone wires
- Fibre-optic cables
- Wireless transmissions
- Satellite links

## 1.1.4 Local Networks

A local network connects devices within a limited geographical area, such as a home, office, school or college.

### Types of Networks

**Small Home Networks**
- Connect personal devices such as smartphones, laptops, tablets and printers.
- Allow resource sharing and internet access.

**SOHO Networks**
- SOHO stands for Small Office/Home Office.
- Designed for small businesses and home offices.
- Allow sharing of files, printers and internet connections.

**Medium to Large Networks**
- Used by businesses and organisations.
- Connect large numbers of computers, servers and other devices.
- Support communication and resource sharing.

**Worldwide Networks**
- Connect networks across different geographical locations.
- The internet is the largest example of interconnected networks.

## 1.1.5 Mobile Devices

Mobile devices are portable computing devices that can connect to the internet.

Examples:

- Smartphones – Communication, browsing, GPS and media.
- Tablets – Browsing, reading and video conferencing.
- Smartwatches – Notifications and activity tracking.
- Smart glasses – Display digital information through wearable devices.

## 1.1.6 Connected Home Devices

Many household devices connect to the internet and can be monitored or controlled remotely.

Examples:

- Security systems – Remote home monitoring.
- Smart appliances – Remote control and monitoring.
- Smart TVs – Streaming online content.
- Gaming consoles – Online multiplayer gaming.

These devices are examples of the Internet of Things (IoT).

## 1.1.7 Other Connected Devices

Internet connectivity is also used in devices outside homes.

### Smart Cars
Connected cars provide navigation, entertainment, maintenance alerts and emergency assistance.

### RFID Tags
RFID stands for Radio Frequency Identification. It uses radio waves to identify and track tagged objects.

### Sensors and Actuators
- Sensors detect environmental changes such as temperature, movement and light.
- Actuators perform physical actions based on control signals.

### Medical Devices
Connected medical devices monitor patient information and transmit readings to healthcare systems.

## Additional Learning: Internet of Things (IoT)

IoT refers to physical devices connected to networks that collect, exchange and sometimes act on data.

Examples include smart cameras, smart TVs, connected cars and smart appliances.

## Cybersecurity Relevance

Connected devices can introduce security risks:

- Unsecured cameras can expose private video feeds.
- Weak router passwords can allow unauthorised access.
- Vulnerable IoT devices can become entry points for attackers.

Securing connected devices is an important part of network security.

## Key Takeaways

1. The internet is a network of networks.
2. No single individual owns the entire internet.
3. Local networks connect devices within limited areas.
4. SOHO networks support small offices and home offices.
5. IoT connects physical devices to networks.
6. Connected devices need proper security.
